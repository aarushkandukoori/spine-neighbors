Can a vertebra borrow from its neighbors?

A toy 2D simulation of level-wise spine CT updating from stereovision. Aarush Kandukoori, Carnegie Mellon, November 2026.

In the stereovision pipelines I read (Cai et al. 2021, Warner et al. 2025), the last step fits each vertebra rigidly to its own surface points. I wanted to see what happens when one level's points are bad or missing, and whether tying each vertebra to its neighbors at the discs helps. Everything here is synthetic and in 2D.

Click a vertebra (or use the buttons under the picture) to change what the camera sees at that level. The slider sets how strongly each disc holds its two vertebrae together.

Side view, animal prone, camera above, head to the left. Gray fill - where the bones really are in the OR. Dots - stereovision points (orange ones sit on soft tissue). Dashed outline - each level fit alone. Blue outline - levels tied at the discs. Short lines inside each body - screw-tip error.

What happens over 300 random spines

At the ends it helps less. L1 and L6 have only one disc, so a bad end level can still turn about it. Soft tissue at an end level drops from 3.1 to 1.7 mm, but with nothing visible there the error only drops from about 23 to 20 mm.

The catch is that the springs only help if they are about as stiff as real discs. The toy's discs slip about 0.4 mm between the CT and the OR. When I let them slip 1.5 mm and kept the same spring, the clean levels got a little worse (about 1.0 to 1.2 mm), while the bad level still improved (3.1 to 1.6 mm with soft tissue). So the stiffness would have to come from real data.

Where this toy is wrong

  • It is 2D. In 3D, a vertebra held only at its two discs can still spin about the line between them, so the facet joints or some of its own surface would have to pin that down. The hidden-level result is the most optimistic one here.
  • Every vertebra has the same made-up shape, and each disc is a simple spring at one point.
  • "Each level alone" here is plain point-to-plane ICP from five starting angles, not the rectification and 2D registration pipeline in the papers.
  • Soft tissue and blocked views are simple stand-ins, and the screw-tip error stands in for fiducial TRE.
  • The posture change (about 48 degrees of lordosis over L1 to L6) follows Fan et al. 2022. The noise levels are my guesses (1 mm per point, plus a 0.7 mm offset per level for tracking and calibration).

What I would try on real data

  1. Measure how much neighboring vertebrae actually slip relative to each other between preoperative CT and intraoperative CT in existing scans. That sets the spring.
  2. Hide or corrupt one level at a time in existing stereovision data and compare each level alone with the tied fit.
  3. Use the gap between a level's own answer and the answer its neighbors imply as a warning that a mask is off. The toy marks a level "check" when the two answers put the screw tip more than 2 mm apart. It needs no ground truth.
